Voices · on-device

Voices that run on the phone.

Two ways to speak: the voice Android already has, or a neural model you install once. Both work offline. Neither sends your text anywhere.

The voice Android already has.

Instant, offline, and available in every language the device supports. There is no download and no licence step: it works on the first launch. Quality depends on the engine the phone ships with, which is the honest trade.

Four neural voices.

  • Supertonic 3 123 MB

    Two curated styles from twenty. The fast default once installed, and the benchmark's speed and memory winner.

  • Kokoro 103 MB

    Two curated English voices from eleven. Audiobook pacing, about 3× slower in absolute synthesis; still the quality option pending a listening test.

  • Pocket TTS 98 MB

    Zero-shot cloning from a short reference clip. Audio only.

  • ZipVoice 104 MB + 52 MB

    Cloning driven by a clip plus a verbatim transcript, Chinese and English. Needs the 24 kHz vocoder.

  • Vocoder 24 kHz 52 MB

    The dependency ZipVoice needs. Nothing else uses it.

How an install works.

  1. You pick a model and confirm the download size. Nothing downloads before that.
  2. The bundle comes from the upstream k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx tts-models release.
  3. Every file is checked against a SHA-256 pinned in the app.
  4. Extraction shows per-file progress and holds a wake lock so the phone does not sleep through it. It takes about one to three minutes; a 45 s no-progress watchdog aborts with a readable error.

Measured, not asserted.

The project measures its own synthesis rather than describing it. The 2026-11-06 story-cast benchmark covers host and on-device real-time factor, speaker-switch overhead, word error rate, pitch separation and an audio-capable judge.

The harness is in the repository, so the numbers can be reproduced on another phone: tools/cast-benchmark and tools/tts-benchmark.

Not measured yet: a listening test for Kokoro's pacing, emotional range, and ultra-short lines, which are the weakest case for the cast parser.

No network after install.

Playback with an installed voice does not touch the network. Previewing a voice does not disturb the active engine, and changing the model updates the live engine instead of reloading it.

The app still needs the network to fetch a chapter you open, because that is the page. What it never does is send the text somewhere to be spoken.
