Chapter Three: The Keeper of Tides

by E. M. Harrow · The Wick Chronicle

The stranger's name was Osric Vane, and he claimed to be a clerk. Mara had known clerks before; they smelled of ink and apology, and they did not stand in inn doorways at night with the sea in their hair. Vane smelled of neither. He ordered nothing, paid for nothing, and yet the innkeeper brought him a glass of something amber without being asked, which in Mara's experience meant either great wealth or great trouble.

"The sixth lamp," Vane said, "is the lamp at Saltsend that has never been lit. There are six lamps on the north pier and five of them burn, and have burned since before your grandfather was born. The sixth has been cold for a hundred and twelve years, and every year the tide comes a little higher. You may have noticed that the sea has been patient with Wick. It will not be patient forever."

Mara thought of her father at the kitchen table, his hands wrapped around a cup, telling her what a lamplighter was for. She thought of the letter on the shelf, and the ship with her lanterns dark, and the way the wind had changed sides. "You could have told me this in Wick," she said. "You did not need to follow me up the coast."

Vane smiled at that, and for the first time he looked less like a clerk and more like something the tide had left behind. "I did not follow you," he said. "I was sent ahead. The keeper of the sixth lamp is not a person, Mistress Hale. It is a post. It has been vacant for a hundred and twelve years because nobody in Saltsend would take it, and the lamps of Wick have been burning in its place, one by one, every night, because your family has been paying a debt it did not know it owed."

Outside, the tide was coming in over the salt pans, quiet and orderly, filling the shallow squares one after another. Mara set her bowl down and stood up and put on her coat, because a lamplighter does not sit still while the dark does its work. "Then I had better see the sixth lamp," she said. Vane bowed, as though she had done exactly what he expected, and opened the door.
